**II. Characteristics & Challenges of Chhattisgarh Agriculture**

- **High Dependence:** ~80% of the population engaged in agriculture & allied activities (~40.1 Lakh farmer families). This creates pressure on the land.
    
- **Dominance of Small & Marginal Farmers:**
    
    - **Marginal Farmers:** Landholding < 1 hectare.
        
    - **Small Farmers:** Landholding 1-2 hectares.
        
    - **Combined:** Small & Marginal farmers (0-2 hectares) constitute **82%** of the total farmer families.
        
    - Implication: Limited resources, economic vulnerability.
        
- **Subsistence Farming:** Due to small landholdings, farming is primarily for self-consumption (Jeevan Nirvah Krishi) rather than commercial purposes.
    
- **Food Crop Dominance:** Emphasis on growing food crops (especially paddy) for self-sufficiency first.
    
- **Credit Issues (Saakh Samasya):** Small farmers face challenges accessing formal credit, often relying on moneylenders.
    
- **Rainfed Agriculture:** High dependency on monsoon rainfall. Irrigation coverage is limited.
    
- **Lack of Modernization:** Predominantly traditional farming methods due to lack of capital among small farmers. Limited use of modern machinery and techniques.
    
- **Soil Fertility:** Soil fertility is generally low in many areas, impacting productivity. Red-yellow soil dominates, but other areas have less productive soil.
    

**III. Agricultural Credit System**

- **NABARD (1982):** Apex institution for agricultural finance, channeling funds.
    
- **State Level Distribution:**
    
    - **Cooperative Banks:**
        
        - **Apex Bank (State Level - HQ: Raipur):** 1
            
        - **Central Cooperative Banks (District Level):** 7 (Ambikapur, Raigarh, Bilaspur, Durg, Rajnandgaon, Jagdalpur, Raipur - Raipur has max branches)
            
        - **Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS - Village Level):** 2058 (as per latest data mentioned) - Direct lending to farmers.
            
    - **Chhattisgarh Rajya Gramin Bank (RRB):** Sponsored by SBI. Ownership: Central Govt (50%), Sponsor Bank (35%), State Govt (15%). Provides wider range of loans beyond just agriculture. Mobile App: M-Tej.
        
- **Loan Interest Rates:**
    
    - **Short-Term Loans (up to 15 months):** 0% interest rate currently for all agriculture & allied activities.
        
    - Paddy loans were made 0% in 2014-15.
        
    - All other agri-related short-term loans made 0% recently (last year mentioned in lecture).

**VII. Crop Classification & Details**

**A. By Season (Based on Agricultural Year: June 1 - May 31)**

1. **Kharif Season (Monsoon Crop): June - Mid-October**
    
    - Conditions: Hot & Humid, requires high rainfall (>100-120 cm).
        
    - Dominance: Major cropping season in CG due to rainfed nature. Covers almost entire cultivable area.
        
    - Major Crops:
        
        - **Paddy (Dhan):** Most dominant crop (60-80% of Kharif area).
            
            - Top Producer: Janjgir-Champa
                
            - Second: Dhamtari (mentioned, verify with latest survey)
                
        - **Maize (Makka):** Second major foodgrain after paddy.
            
            - Top Producer: Balrampur (mentioned, verify)
                
            - Second: Kondagaon (mentioned, verify)
                
        - **Kodo-Kutki, Ragi (Millets):** Important in tribal/upland areas.
            
        - **Jowar (Sorghum)**
            
        - **Soybean:** Important oilseed.
            
            - Top Producer: Rajnandgaon (mentioned, verify)
                
        - **Groundnut (Moongfali):**
            
            - Top Producer: Raigarh (also significant in Mungeli).
                
        - **Arhar (Tur/Pigeon Pea):** Pulse crop.
            
        - **Cotton (Kapas):** Minor cultivation (e.g., Durg area).
            
        - **Jute Substitutes (Mesta, Sanai):** Fibre crops, mainly in Raigarh.
            
        - **Sugarcane (Ganna):**
            
            - Top Producer: Kabirdham (Kawardha)
                
2. **Rabi Season (Winter Crop): Mid-October - February**
    
    - Conditions: Cool & Less Humid, lower water requirement (50-100 cm), often requires irrigation.
        
    - Dominant Areas: Regions with irrigation/black soil (Bemetara, Rajnandgaon, Kabirdham).
        
    - Major Crops:
        
        - **Wheat (Gehu):**
            
            - Top Producer: Rajnandgaon (mentioned, verify)
                
        - **Chickpea (Chana):** Most important Rabi pulse.
            
            - Top Producer: Bemetara
                
        - **Mustard (Sarson):** Major oilseed by area.
            
            - Top Area: (Statewide)
                
            - Top Producer: Bemetara / Rajnandgaon belt likely strong.
                
        - **Linseed (Alsi):** Oilseed.
            
        - **Tivra/Lakhdi (Lathyrus):** Pulse crop.
            
        - **Peas (Matar):** Pulse/Vegetable.
            
        - **Sunflower (Suryamukhi):** Oilseed.
            
        - **Sesame (Til), Niger (Ram Til):** Oilseeds.
            
        - **Barley (Jau)**
            
3. **Zaid Season (Summer Crop): March - May/June**
    
    - Conditions: Short season, requires assured irrigation/river banks.
        
    - Major Crops: Cucumber, Watermelon, Muskmelon, Vegetables.

**IX. Agro-Climatic Zones of Chhattisgarh**

- India Context: India has 15 Agro-Climatic Zones. Chhattisgarh falls under the **Eastern Plateau and Hills Region**.
    
- Chhattisgarh Sub-division (3 Zones):
    
    1. **Northern Hills Zone (Uttar ka Uchh Bhoomi/Maidan):**
        
        - Area: ~22%
            
        - Rainfall: Lowest (~1200-1250 mm).
            
        - Soil: Mainly Laterite, some Red-Yellow.
            
        - Crops: Horticulture (Tomato, Potato), Maize (Balrampur), Oilseeds (Sunflower - Surajpur), Millets.
            
    2. **Chhattisgarh Plains (Madhya ka Maidani Kshetra):**
        
        - Area: Largest (~50%).
            
        - Rainfall: Moderate (~1450 mm).
            
        - Soil: Diverse - Red-Yellow (dominant), Black soil, Dorasa, Kanhar, Matasi.
            
        - Crops: Hub of CG agriculture - Paddy, Pulses, Oilseeds, Sugarcane - almost all major crops grown here.
            
    3. **Bastar Plateau (Dakshin ka Pathari Kshetra):**
        
        - Area: ~28%.
            
        - Rainfall: Highest (~1500 mm+).
            
        - Soil: Red Sandy/Gravelly (Tikra), Red Loamy (Mal), Dorasa. Some fertile loam (Domat) in Sabari-Godavari plains.
            
        - Crops: Millets (Kodo, Kutki, Ragi), Pulses, Oilseeds, Maize, Horticulture, Forest produce based.
