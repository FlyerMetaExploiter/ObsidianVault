**1. Introduction & Context**

- **National Forest Policy (1952) Target:** Aims for 1/3rd (approx. 33%) of India's total land area to be under forest cover.
    
- **India's Average Forest Cover:** Approximately 24%.
    
- **Chhattisgarh's Forest Cover:**
    
    - According to State Forest Dept: Approx. 44%.
        
    - According to India State of Forest Report (ISFR, previous report): Approx. 41% (needs latest ISFR update).
        
    - Chhattisgarh's forest cover is significantly higher than the national average and the policy target.
        
- **Ranking (based on previous ISFR):**
    
    - By Forest Area: 3rd in India (after MP, Arunachal Pradesh).
        
    - By Forest Cover: 4th in India.
        
    - Highest % Forest Cover (State's own area): Mizoram.
        
    - Highest Forest Cover (Total Area): Madhya Pradesh.
        

**2. Forest Classification in Chhattisgarh**

Chhattisgarh's forests are classified based on different criteria:

**A. Based on Climate & Vegetation (Champion & Seth Classification):**

- **Primary Type:** Tropical Deciduous Forests (Parnapati Van). Trees shed leaves during a specific season.
    
- **Sub-Types:**
    
    - **Moist Deciduous (Aadr Parnapati):**
        
        - Approx. 47% of CG's forest area.
            
        - Representative Tree: Sal (Shorea robusta).
            
        - **Distribution:** Found in regions with higher rainfall like Dandakaranya Plateau, Eastern Marginal Highlands (Simant Uchchabhumi), and Eastern parts of Jashpur (Pat region).
            
    - **Dry Deciduous (Shushk Parnapati):**
        
        - Approx. 51% of CG's forest area (Dominant type).
            
        - Representative Tree: Teak (Sagoun / Tectona grandis). Other associated trees include Tendu, Mahua, Palash, Khair etc.
            
        - **Distribution:** Covers the remaining areas, especially prevalent in the Mahanadi River basin and areas with moderate rainfall (100-120 cm).
            

**B. Based on Dominant Tree Species:**

- **Sal Forests:**
    
    - Area: Approx. 40.56% (24,244 sq km mentioned, need verification with latest data).
        
    - **Distribution:** Concentrated in the southern region (Dandakaranya - often called 'Island of Sal'), Maikal Range, Jashpur region, Marginal Highlands (Barnawapara, Sitanadi). Keskal & Machkot valleys known for dense Sal.
        
- **Teak (Sagoun) Forests:**
    
    - Area: Approx. 9.4%.
        
    - **Distribution:** Best quality found in Kurschel Valley (Narayanpur). Also found scattered in Maikal Range, Achanakmar area (Mungeli, Pendra, Lormi plateau), and some parts of the plains.
        
- **Mixed Forests:**
    
    - Area: Approx. 43.52% (Largest category by area).
        
    - **Distribution:** Predominantly found in the central Chhattisgarh plains (Mahanadi Basin).
        
    - **Composition:** Includes a variety of trees like Khair, Tendu, Mahua, Palash, Bija, Saja, Bel, Bamboo, etc.
        
- **Bamboo Forests:**
    
    - Area: Approx. 6,556 sq km (approx. 6% of total forest area, often considered within Mixed Forests).
        
    - Accounts for ~11% of India's bamboo production.
        
    - Significant economic contribution: ~29% of the value of Minor Forest Produce (MFP) in CG, ~12% of state's forest revenue.
        
    - **Distribution:** Found along border districts, particularly Sarguja, Korba, Raigarh, GPM, Mungeli.
        
    - **Types:** Lathi Bans, Katangi Bans.
        

**C. Based on Administrative / Legal Status:**

- **Reserved Forests (Aarakshit Van):**
    
    - Area: Approx. 43.13%.
        
    - Highest level of protection; most activities prohibited unless permitted.
        
    - **Distribution:** Maximum area in Dantewada district, Minimum area in Korba district.
        
- **Protected Forests (Sanrakshit Van):**
    
    - Area: Approx. 40.2%.
        
    - Protection level is lower than Reserved; some local rights allowed, commercial exploitation restricted.
        
    - **Distribution:** Maximum area in Surguja district, Minimum area in Janjgir-Champa district.
        
- **Unclassified Forests (Avargikrit Van):**
    
    - Area: Approx. 16.65%.
        
    - Managed by revenue departments, lowest protection, open access.
        
    - **Distribution:** Maximum area reported in Dantewada district, Minimum area in Dhamtari district.
        

**3. Key Forest Resources & Related Aspects**

- **Major Forest Produce (Mukhya Vanopaj):** Primarily Timber.
    
    - Identified Trees: 13 species identified for timber.
        
    - **Nationalized Timber Trees (6):** Sal, Teak (Sagoun), Saja, Bija, Shisham, Khair. These are commercially important woods.
        
- **Minor Forest Produce (Lagu Vanopaj - MFP):** Non-timber items.
    
    - Includes leaves, seeds, fruits, gums, resins, honey, lac, bamboo, medicinal plants etc.
        
    - **Management:** Collection often linked to tribal livelihoods, managed under Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA) via schemes like MSP for MFP (started 2013-14, CG procurement from 2014-15). Nodal agency: TRIFED at center. State agency: CG MFP (Trading & Development) Cooperative Federation.
        
    - **Number Procured under MSP in CG:** 72 (as per lecture, subject to change).
        
    - **Key MFPs:**
        
        - **Tendu Patta (Diospyros melanoxylon leaves):** "Green Gold", used for Bidi rolling. Nationalized MFP. CG is 2nd largest producer (after Jharkhand). Contributes ~17% of MFP revenue. MSP rate mentioned (subject to govt policy changes). Major area: Bastar.
            
        - **Sal Seed & Leaves:** Also collected as MFP.
            
        - **Mahua (Madhuca longifolia):** Flowers & seeds used (culturally significant, food, local liquor). Dry deciduous tree.
            
        - **Imli (Tamarind):** Major production in Bastar (largest mandi in Jagdalpur). Processing center proposed in Bastar.
            
        - **Harra (Terminalia chebula):** Seed used for medicinal purposes. Processing unit in Kanker (Nathiya Nava Gaon).
            
        - **Honey:** Processing centers in Bilaspur (near Kanan Pendari), Kanker (Bhanupratappur), Kawardha, Jashpur. Purest honey region mentioned: Ama Dob village (Achanakmar-Amarkantak).
            
        - **Lac:** Resin secreted by an insect (Kerria lacca) on host trees (Palash, Ber, Kusum). Used for bangles, varnish. Given 'Agriculture' status in CG (first state). Processing centers: Rajnandgaon, Jashpur. Training center: Makdi (Kondagaon). Production regions: Raipur, Bilaspur, Surguja, Raigarh.
            
        - **Bamboo:** (Details in Sec 2B)
            
        - **Khair (Acacia catechu):** Bark used to extract Kattha (Catechu), used in Paan. Extraction mainly by Kharwar tribe. Found in Surguja, Bilaspur, Bastar.
            
        - **Others:** Chironji, Kullu Gum, Phool Jhadu (broom grass), Neem seeds, Amla, Baheda, Medicinal herbs.
            
- **Medicinal Plants:** White Musli, Sarpgandha, Tulsi (Sweet Basil - Kondagaon cultivation).
    

**4. Key Wildlife Associated with Forests**

- **State Bird: Bastar Hill Myna (Gracula religiosa peninsularis):**
    
    - Largest among the 4 Indian Hill Myna subspecies.
        
    - Omnivore.
        
    - Features: Black body, orange beak, yellow wattles (skin flaps near eyes), white patches on wings ('mirror').
        
    - Known as the best mimic of human voice (termed by Dr. Salim Ali). This ability also makes it vulnerable to poaching.
        
    - **Distribution/Conservation:** Primarily found and conserved in Kanger Valley National Park. Declared State Bird in 2001.
        
- **State Animal: Wild Buffalo (Bubalus arnee / Bubalus bubalis in lecture):**
    
    - Scientific Name: Bubalus arnee (Asiatic Wild Buffalo). (Note: B. bubalis is the domestic water buffalo).
        
    - Purest breed found in Chhattisgarh.
        
    - Features: Mostly black, white 'stockings' on lower legs. Herbivore. Approx weight 250kg+.
        
    - **Distribution/Conservation:** Primarily found in Udanti-Sitanadi Tiger Reserve and Indravati National Park. Conservation breeding efforts also at Achanakmar and Jungle Safari (Naya Raipur).
        
    - Threats: Habitat loss, Rinderpest disease, Poaching (tendency to stay in plains makes it vulnerable).
        
    - Translocation project from Assam underway to improve genetic diversity.
        
    - Mentioned in historical text 'Prithviraj Raso'.
        
- **Tigers:**
    
    - Current count: 17 (as per lecture, needs update from latest census). Declined from 19 (previous census) and 44 (census before that).
        
    - Part of Western/Central India Tiger Landscape.
        
    - **Distribution:** Highest density in Achanakmar Tiger Reserve. Also present in Indravati, Udanti-Sitanadi, Guru Ghasidas National Park.
        
    - **Corridors:** Kanha-Achanakmar corridor development proposed/underway. Connects to larger corridors (Bhoramdeo, Tadoba, Bandhavgarh etc.).
        
    - **Project Tiger Sites in CG:** Achanakmar, Indravati, Udanti-Sitanadi, Guru Ghasidas NP (+ Tamor Pingla WLS).
        
- **Other Mentions:**
    
    - **Armadillo:** South American native, first sighting reported in Manendragarh region.
        
    - **Elephant Corridors:** Three pass through Chhattisgarh. Human-elephant conflict is an issue (Mahua mentioned as a factor).
        

**5. Forest Administration Structure**

- **Apex Body:** State Forest Department (Van Vibhag).
    
- **Chhattisgarh State Forest Development Corporation (CGSFDC):** Established May 2001. Headed by Chief Minister (as per lecture).
    
- **Hierarchy (Field):**
    
    - Principal Chief Conservator of Forests (PCCF) - Head of Forest Force
        
    - Chief Conservator of Forests (CCF) - Heads Forest Circle
        
    - Divisional Forest Officer (DFO) - Heads Forest Division
        
    - Sub-Divisional Forest Officer (SDFO) / Assistant Conservator of Forests (ACF) - Heads Sub-Division
        
    - Range Officer (Ranger) - Heads Forest Range
        
    - Deputy Ranger - Heads Sub-Range/Section
        
    - Forest Guard - Heads Forest Beat
        
- **Administrative Units (Approx. numbers mentioned in lecture, may vary):**
    
    - **Forest Circles (Van Vritt):** 6 (Bilaspur - largest, Durg - smallest, Jagdalpur, Surguja, Raipur, Kanker). Headed by CCF.
        
    - **Forest Divisions (Van Mandal):** 34. Headed by DFO.
        
    - **Forest Sub-Divisions (Up Van Mandal):** 82. Headed by SDFO.
        
    - **Forest Ranges (Van Parikshetra):** 320. Headed by Ranger.
        
    - **Sub-Ranges:** 872. Headed by Deputy Ranger.
        
    - **Forest Beats (Van Beat):** 3287. Managed by Forest Guard.
        

**6. Conservation Efforts & Centers (Brief Mention)**

- **Protected Areas:** National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserve (Achanakmar-Amarkantak).
    
- **Zoos:** Kanan Pendari (Bilaspur), Nandan Van (Raipur), Maitri Bagh (Bhilai - managed by SAIL).
    
- **Jungle Safari:** Naya Raipur (1st), Kawardha (2nd proposed).
    
- **Bird Sanctuaries/Watching Areas:** Gidhwa-Parsada (Bemetara - held 1st Bird Festival 2021), Rangi (Bemetara), Patan (Durg), Bilasa Taal (Bilaspur), Kanki (Korba).
    
- **Crocodile Conservation:** Kotmi Sonar (Janjgir-Champa), Bhaisa Darha (Kanger Valley NP), Jatatundi (Narayanpur).
    
- **Game Sanctuary:** Kutru (Bijapur).
    
- **Programs/Boards:** CAMPA (Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority), State Medicinal Plants Board.
    
- **State Forest Policy:** Currently following the 2001 policy.
