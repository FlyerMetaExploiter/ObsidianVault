
---

**1. Introduction** Chhattisgarh showcases a rich geological heritage, representing nearly all major periods of Indian geological history. The state's rocks range from some of the oldest in India, found in the Bastar region, to younger sedimentary and volcanic formations. This diverse geology makes the state rich in natural resources like coal, iron ore, and bauxite.

---

**2. Archean: Bastar Craton (Ancient Basement Rocks)**

- The Bastar Craton forms the oldest part of Chhattisgarh's geology, more than 3 billion years old.
    
- It consists mainly of ancient rocks like granites and gneisses (types of very old, hard rocks formed deep inside the Earth).
    
- These rocks are the foundation over which younger rocks were later deposited.
    
- The region is rich in minerals such as iron (Bailadila), tin (Sukma-Dantewada), and gold.
    

---

**3. Dharwar-Type Rocks (Greenstone Belts and Iron Formations)**

- These rocks are similar to those found in the Dharwar region of Karnataka.
    
- They include volcanic rocks, schists (layered rocks), and iron-rich formations.
    
- Banded Iron Formations (BIFs) found in Bailadila are important for iron ore mining today.
    
- These rocks tell us about the early oceans and atmosphere.
    

---

**4. Chhattisgarh Supergroup (Equivalent to Cuddapah Basin)**

- These rocks were formed over the Bastar Craton during the Proterozoic era (around 1.6 to 1 billion years ago).
    
- They are divided into two main parts:
    
    - **Chilpi Group**: Sandstones and limestones found in the western parts.
        
    - **Raipur Group**: Includes limestones, dolomites, and shales found in central Chhattisgarh.
        
- The presence of **stromatolites** (fossils formed by microorganisms) indicates early forms of life.
    
- These rocks formed in shallow seas and were influenced by tectonic activity like faulting.
    

---

**5. Vindhyan Rocks (Northern Chhattisgarh)**

- Found in the northern districts, these rocks belong to the Vindhyan Supergroup.
    
- Made up of horizontal layers of sandstone and shale.
    
- These were deposited in stable conditions and are younger than the Chhattisgarh Supergroup.
    
- The **Son-Narmada Lineament**, a major fault zone, lies near these rocks and is still geologically active.
    

---

**6. Gondwana Supergroup (Coal-Bearing Rocks)**

- Gondwana rocks were deposited in river basins during the Permian period (~250 million years ago).
    
- Found in coalfields like Hasdeo-Arand, Mand-Raigarh, and Tatapani-Ramkola.
    
- They contain sandstones, shales, and thick coal seams.
    
- Fossils of ancient plants like **Glossopteris** are found, showing that the area once had lush forests.
    

---

**7. Deccan Trap and Dyke Swarms (Volcanic Activity)**

- While the main Deccan volcanic rocks are in western India, Chhattisgarh has some volcanic features too.
    
- **Mafic dykes**, which are vertical rock bodies formed by volcanic activity, are found near Rajnandgaon and Durg.
    
- These intrusions happened during the time of Deccan volcanism (~65 million years ago).
    

---

**8. Tectonic Features: Lineaments and Mobile Belts**

- Chhattisgarh lies near major geological fault lines:
    
    - **Son-Narmada Lineament (SNL)**: A long fault zone active since ancient times.
        
    - **Kotri–Dongargarh Mobile Belt**: A zone of crustal movement and ancient earthquakes.
        
- These tectonic zones influenced the formation of sedimentary basins and mineral deposits.
    

---

**9. Mineral Wealth of Chhattisgarh**

- Due to its diverse geology, Chhattisgarh is rich in various minerals:
    
    - **Iron ore**: Bailadila, Rowghat
        
    - **Coal**: Hasdeo-Arand, Mand-Raigarh
        
    - **Bauxite**: Kawardha, Surguja
        
    - **Tin**: Dantewada (rare in India)
        
    - Also has limestone, manganese, and gold
        

---

**10. Paleoclimate and Fossils**

- Stromatolites in the Chhattisgarh Supergroup give clues about early life and marine environments.
    
- Glossopteris fossils in Gondwana rocks point to a cold and moist ancient climate.
    
- These fossils help reconstruct ancient climates and ecosystems.
    

---

**11. Conclusion** Chhattisgarh's geology is a journey through time — from ancient rocks of the Bastar Craton to fossil-rich Gondwana and volcanic dykes. The diversity of rock types and structures not only makes the region geologically special but also economically important due to its rich mineral resources. This layered geological history gives deep insight into Earth’s evolution in central India.
