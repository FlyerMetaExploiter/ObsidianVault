### **Condition of Labour Class during British Rule**

The condition of the labour class in British India was highly exploitative, marked by poor wages, hazardous working conditions, and minimal legal protection. The introduction of modern industries led to a shift from agrarian occupations, but this transformation largely benefited the British rulers and Indian elites while worsening the plight of workers.

---

### **1. Exploitation in Agricultural Sector**

- **Land Revenue Policies:** Heavy taxation under systems like Permanent Settlement (1793), Ryotwari, and Mahalwari pushed peasants into debt.
- **Indebtedness and Bonded Labour:** Farmers frequently fell into the trap of moneylenders, leading to bonded labour and loss of land.
- **Forced Cultivation:** British policies forced farmers to grow commercial crops like indigo, tea, and jute instead of food crops, leading to frequent famines.

---

### **2. Harsh Conditions in Industrial Sector**

- **Emergence of Factory System:** With the rise of textile mills, coal mines, and railways, many rural workers moved to industrial jobs but faced severe exploitation.
- **Poor Wages and Long Hours:** Labourers worked for 12–16 hours a day with meagre wages, barely enough for subsistence.
- **Lack of Safety Measures:** Industrial accidents were frequent, with no medical benefits or compensation for injuries.
- **Child and Women Labour:** Women and children were extensively employed in factories and mines under harsh conditions, often receiving lower wages than men.
-  **Poor Working Conditions**: Factories were unsafe, with no ventilation or sanitation facilities.

---

### **3. Working Conditions in Plantations and Mines**

- **Indentured Labour System:** Workers were forcibly recruited and sent to British colonies like Fiji, Mauritius, and the Caribbean under exploitative contracts.
- **Tea and Indigo Plantations:** Labourers on plantations worked under strict supervision with low wages and in inhuman living conditions.
- **Hazardous Work in Mines:** The mining sector, especially in Bengal and Jharkhand, exposed workers to severe health risks with no safety provisions.
- - **No Legal Rights**: Labourers had no legal protection or rights to organize.

---

### **4. Lack of Labour Rights and Legal Protections**

- **Absence of Labour Laws:** Until the late 19th century, there were no laws regulating working hours, wages, or safety.
- **First Labour Laws:** The Factories Act of 1881 and later in 1891 provided minimal reforms, but they were poorly implemented.
- **No Right to Unionize:** British authorities discouraged trade unions, and striking workers faced repression.

---

### **5. Economic Hardships and Famine Impact**

- **Deindustrialization:** British policies destroyed traditional handicrafts, forcing artisans into low-paying industrial jobs or poverty.
- **Famine and Malnutrition:** Famines like the Bengal Famine of 1943 worsened the condition of workers, with minimal government relief.
- **Inflation and Wage Stagnation:** Prices of essential goods rose, but wages remained stagnant, leading to widespread poverty.

---

### **6. Growth of Labour Movements**

- **Early Resistance:** Initial protests were scattered and often crushed by colonial authorities.
- **Formation of Trade Unions:** The first organized efforts, such as the Madras Labour Union (1918), demanded better wages and conditions.
- **Impact of Nationalist Movements:** Leaders like Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar supported labour rights, linking them with the freedom struggle.

---

### **7.Urban Labour**:
   - **Unemployment**: High unemployment due to deindustrialization under British policies.
   - **Migration**: Rural workers migrated to cities for work, leading to urban slums.
   - **No Social Security**: No pensions, healthcare, or job security for workers.
   

---

   - ### **8.Peasant Movements**:
    
     **Indigo Revolt (1859-60)**: Peasants in Bengal revolted against forced indigo cultivation.
     **Deccan Riots (1875)**: Peasants in Maharashtra protested against moneylenders.
     **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)**: Led by Gandhi, it protested against exploitative indigo plantation systems.

---

### **Conclusion**

The British colonial economy prioritized revenue extraction, leading to the extreme exploitation of the labour class. Despite the gradual emergence of labour laws and trade unions, the working class remained in distress. The struggle for workers' rights became an integral part of the broader nationalist movement, setting the stage for post-independence labour reforms.
