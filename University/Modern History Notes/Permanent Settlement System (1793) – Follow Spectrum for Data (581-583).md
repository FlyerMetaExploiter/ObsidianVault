The **Permanent Settlement** was introduced by **Lord Cornwallis** in **1793** in Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, and parts of Madras and Varanasi. It was designed to provide the **British East India Company** with a **fixed and stable revenue** while granting **Zamindars hereditary ownership** of land. However, it resulted in severe **exploitation of peasants** and stagnation in agriculture.

## **Key Features of the Permanent Settlement**

### **1. Permanent Fixation of Revenue (Taxation System)**

- The **land revenue was permanently fixed** and could **never be increased or decreased**.
- The tax was **set at a very high rate**, often equal to **10/11th (90%) of the total agricultural produce**.
- Zamindars had to **pay this fixed tax annually to the British government**.
- Any **failure to pay on time** resulted in the **auction of their land**.

### **2. Role of Zamindars (Landlords) in Tax Collection**

- Zamindars were officially **recognized as hereditary landowners**.
- They acted as **intermediaries**, collecting revenue from peasants and giving it to the British.
- They were free to **charge higher taxes** from the peasants for their profit.

### **3. Peasants' Tax Burden and Exploitation**

- **Peasants (Ryots) were not considered landowners** and had no legal rights over their land.
- The **fixed high tax** meant peasants had to **pay the same amount** even during **famines or bad harvests**.
- Many peasants **fell into debt**, lost their land, and became **bonded laborers**.

## **Why Did the Permanent Settlement Fail?**

### **1. Unrealistic Taxation**

- The **high tax rate (90%)** was unmanageable for many **Zamindars and peasants**.
- Unlike in England, where tax was **proportionate to agricultural output**, here it was **fixed** and did not consider economic downturns.

### **2. Agricultural Neglect**

- Since Zamindars were **only revenue collectors**, they had **no incentive** to improve irrigation or farming techniques.
- **Land productivity remained low**, leading to frequent **crop failures and famines**.

### **3. Frequent Land Auctions**

- Many Zamindars **failed to pay their taxes** due to high fixed rates.
- Their lands were **auctioned**, leading to instability in ownership.

### **4. Worsening Condition of Peasants**

- **No land rights**, **high taxes**, and **forced evictions** led to widespread **rural distress**.
- This resulted in **large-scale poverty** and migration to **urban centers or other regions**.

## **Conclusion**

The **Permanent Settlement** ensured a **stable revenue** for the British but caused **extreme hardship** for the Indian peasants. The **high fixed tax rate (90%)**, combined with **no flexibility during poor harvests**, led to **economic ruin, land alienation, and widespread poverty**.

Later, the British introduced **Ryotwari and Mahalwari systems**, which were **more flexible and directly involved peasants**, but the **damage caused by Permanent Settlement remained irreversible**.

Thus, the Permanent Settlement **failed as a long-term economic policy**, creating a class of **wealthy landlords** but leaving millions of **peasants impoverished and landless**.
