## **1. Introduction**

- Before British rule, women faced severe social discrimination due to rigid patriarchal norms.
- Practices like **Sati, child marriage, female infanticide**, and **purdah system** were widespread.
- Education and economic opportunities for women were almost non-existent.
- British colonial rule, along with social reform movements, played a crucial role in improving women’s conditions.

## **2. Social Conditions**

### **a) Sati System**

- The practice of **Sati** (widow burning) was prevalent, especially among upper-caste Hindus.
- Reformers like **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** fought against it.
- **Regulation XVII of 1829** (Bengal Sati Regulation) banned Sati under Governor-General **Lord William Bentinck**.

### **b) Child Marriage**

- Girls were married off at an extremely young age, leading to early motherhood and health issues.
- **The Age of Consent Act (1891)** raised the minimum age of marriage to **12 years** for girls.
- Later, **The Sarda Act (Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929)** increased it to **14 years** (later revised in independent India).

### **c) Widow Remarriage**

- Widows, especially in upper castes, were forced into a life of isolation.
- **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** championed widow remarriage.
- **The Hindu Widow Remarriage Act, 1856**, was passed to legalize widow remarriage.

### **d) Purdah System and Seclusion**

- The purdah system restricted women’s mobility and participation in society.
- It was more prevalent in North India among Muslims and upper-caste Hindus.
- Reformers advocated against it, but it remained in practice, especially in conservative families.

## **3. Educational Reforms**

- **Traditional exclusion:** Education for women was discouraged in pre-British India.
- **Christian missionaries** and **Indian reformers** worked to establish girls' schools.
- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** and **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** pushed for female education.
- **Bethune School (1849)** in Calcutta was the first women’s school.
- **Wood’s Dispatch (1854)** emphasized women's education.
- Universities started admitting women in the **late 19th century** (e.g., Calcutta University).
-The British introduced measures o promote education for both men and women, Leading to the establishment of schools an colleges for girls. Missionary efforts. as well as initiatives
by Indian reformers, played a crucial role in expanding educational opportunities for women.
The emphasis on education gradually opened up opportunities for women in various fields,
including teaching. nursing. and social work. challenging traditional gender roles.

## **4. Political Participation and Rights**

- Initially, women had no role in politics.
- The **Government of India Act (1919)** and **Government of India Act (1935)** gradually introduced women’s suffrage.
- **Sarojini Naidu** and **Annie Besant** played key roles in women’s political awakening.
- **Women’s organizations**, such as the **All India Women’s Conference (AIWC)**, emerged to advocate for women's rights.

## **5. Economic Condition**

- Women were largely confined to household work and **not part of the formal economy**.
- **Agriculture and handicrafts** were common areas where women worked informally.
- The **Industrial Revolution and British economic policies** impacted women's traditional occupations, such as weaving.
- Some educated women started working as **teachers, nurses, and clerks** in the late 19th century.

## **6. Property And Inheritance Rights 

British law sought to address the issue of women's property rights, particularly Hindu widows' inheritance rights. Acts such as the Hindu Widow's Remarriage Act of 1856 and the Hindu Succession Act of 1874 aimed to provide women with greater financial security and autonomy by allowing them to inherit property.

## **7. Reform Movements and Key Personalities**

### **a) Raja Ram Mohan Roy**

- Fought against Sati and supported female education.
- Founded **Brahmo Samaj**, which promoted gender equality.

### **b) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**

- Advocated for widow remarriage and female education.
- Played a key role in passing the **Widow Remarriage Act (1856)**.

### **c) Swami Dayanand Saraswati**

- Through **Arya Samaj**, he promoted the **right of women to education and remarriage**.
### **d) Mahatma Gandhi**
- Encouraged women’s participation in the **freedom struggle**.
- Advocated for the removal of **untouchability and child marriage**.
### **e) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**

- Championed women’s rights, especially among **Dalit women**.
- Worked on legal reforms related to **inheritance and marriage laws**.

## **8. British Government’s Role in Women’s Reforms**

- The British initially **avoided interference** in social customs.
- Later, they introduced laws against **Sati, child marriage, and female infanticide**.
- Women were given **access to education and limited political rights**.
- However, many reforms were due to **Indian reformers rather than British efforts**.
- • Legal Protection and Criminal Law: The British introduced legal reforms aimed at providing protection to women against crimes such as domestic violence, rape, and abduction. The Indian Penal Code (IPC) enacted in 1860 Included provisions to address crimes against women However, the enforcement of these Laws varied, and access to justice often depended on factors such as social status, caste, and economic background, Despite these legal protections, many women continued to face barriers to seeking Justice due to societal stigma and discrimination.

## **9. Conclusion**

- The condition of women improved during British rule due to **social reform movements, legal changes, and access to education**.
- Despite progress, **patriarchal attitudes persisted**, and **true equality** was not achieved.
- Women’s active participation in the **freedom struggle** paved the way for greater empowerment in independent India.
