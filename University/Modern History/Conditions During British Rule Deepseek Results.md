### **Condition of Labour During British Rule**

1. **Exploitation in Industries**:
   - **Low Wages**: Workers were paid minimal wages, often below subsistence levels.
   - **Long Hours**: Workdays extended up to 14-16 hours with no overtime pay.
   - **Poor Working Conditions**: Factories were unsafe, with no ventilation or sanitation facilities.
   - **Child Labour**: Children were employed in hazardous conditions, particularly in textile mills.

2. **Plantation Workers**:
   - **Indentured Labour**: Workers, especially in tea plantations, were treated like bonded labourers.
   - **Harsh Living Conditions**: Workers lived in overcrowded, unsanitary housing.
   - **No Legal Rights**: Labourers had no legal protection or rights to organize.

3. **Urban Labour**:
   - **Unemployment**: High unemployment due to deindustrialization under British policies.
   - **Migration**: Rural workers migrated to cities for work, leading to urban slums.
   - **No Social Security**: No pensions, healthcare, or job security for workers.

**Keywords**: Exploitation, low wages, long hours, child labour, indentured labour, unemployment, migration, no legal rights.

### **Condition of Women During British Rule**

1. **Social Status**:
   - **Patriarchal Society**: Women were largely confined to domestic roles.
   - **Illiteracy**: High rates of illiteracy among women due to lack of education.
   - **Sati and Child Marriage**: Practices like Sati and child marriage were prevalent, though reforms were introduced later.

2. **Economic Exploitation**:
   - **Low Wages**: Women in industries and agriculture were paid less than men.
   - **Unorganized Labour**: Women were often employed in unorganized sectors with no job security.

3. **Social Reforms**:
   - **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**: Advocated for women’s rights, leading to the abolition of Sati in 1829.
   - **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**: Promoted widow remarriage and education for women.
   - **Women’s Education**: Missionaries and reformers pushed for girls’ education, though progress was slow.

4. **Participation in Freedom Struggle**:
   - **Role in Movements**: Women like Sarojini Naidu, Annie Besant, and Kasturba Gandhi played key roles in the independence movement.
   - **Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience**: Women participated in large numbers in these movements.

**Keywords**: Patriarchy, illiteracy, Sati, child marriage, low wages, social reforms, women’s education, freedom struggle.

### **Condition of Peasantry During British Rule**

1. **Economic Exploitation**:
   - **High Land Revenue**: Peasants were burdened with high taxes, often leading to debt.
   - **Zamindari System**: Exploitation by zamindars (landlords) who extracted excessive rent.
   - **Commercialization of Agriculture**: Forced cultivation of cash crops like indigo and cotton led to food shortages.

2. **Indebtedness**:
   - **Moneylenders**: Peasants borrowed from moneylenders at high interest rates, leading to a cycle of debt.
   - **Loss of Land**: Many peasants lost their land due to inability to repay loans.

3. **Famines**:
   - **Policy Neglect**: British policies prioritized revenue collection over famine relief.
   - **Severe Famines**: Famines like the Bengal Famine (1943) caused millions of deaths due to lack of government intervention.

4. **Peasant Movements**:
   - **Indigo Revolt (1859-60)**: Peasants in Bengal revolted against forced indigo cultivation.
   - **Deccan Riots (1875)**: Peasants in Maharashtra protested against moneylenders.
   - **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)**: Led by Gandhi, it protested against exploitative indigo plantation systems.

**Keywords**: High land revenue, zamindari system, commercialization of agriculture, indebtedness, famines, peasant movements, Indigo Revolt, Champaran Satyagraha.

### **Summary of Important Points**:
- **Labour**: Exploitation, low wages, child labour, no legal rights.
- **Women**: Patriarchy, social reforms, participation in freedom struggle.
- **Peasantry**: High taxes, indebtedness, famines, peasant revolts.
