The **Mahalwari System** was a revenue collection system introduced during British rule in India. It was implemented mainly in **North-Western India** as a modification of the **Permanent Settlement** and **Ryotwari System**.

## **Introduction**

- Introduced by **Holt Mackenzie** in **1822** and later modified by **Lord William Bentinck in 1833**.
- Implemented in **North-Western Provinces, Punjab, Awadh, Madhya Pradesh, and parts of Uttar Pradesh**.
- The word **"Mahal"** means **village** or **estate**.
- Unlike the **Permanent Settlement**, the revenue demand was **periodically revised**.

## **Key Features of the Mahalwari System**

1. **Revenue Settlement with the Village or Mahal**
    
    - Revenue was settled with **entire villages** (Mahal) instead of individuals.
    - The responsibility for tax payment was collectively on the **village headmen (Lambardar) or landlords**.
2. **Periodic Revision of Revenue**
    
    - Unlike the **Permanent Settlement**, which was **fixed**, the revenue demand in Mahalwari was **revised periodically (every 20-30 years)**.
3. **Assessment Based on Land Productivity**
    
    - The revenue was determined based on **the estimated produce of the land** and its **productive capacity**.
4. **Government’s Share of Revenue**
    
    - Initially set at **66% of the produce**, but later reduced due to farmer protests.
5. **Collective Responsibility**
    
    - The entire village community was responsible for **paying revenue**.
    - If any farmer failed, others had to compensate.

## **Implementation Areas**

- **North-Western Provinces (present-day Uttar Pradesh)**
- **Punjab**
- **Madhya Pradesh**
- **Delhi**
- **Awadh (present-day Uttar Pradesh parts)**

## **Impact of the Mahalwari System**

### **Positive Effects**

- Ensured a **stable source of revenue** for the British.
- Allowed **flexibility** compared to the **Permanent Settlement**.
- Helped in the **classification and assessment** of agricultural land.

### **Negative Effects**

- High tax rates **led to farmer exploitation**.
- Frequent **revisions** caused **economic instability**.
- The **Lambardar (village headman)** often misused power, leading to **corruption**.
- Farmers were often forced to take **loans from moneylenders**, leading to **indebtedness**.

## **Comparison with Other Revenue Systems**

|Feature|Mahalwari System|Permanent Settlement|Ryotwari System|
|---|---|---|---|
|Introduced By|Holt Mackenzie (1822)|Lord Cornwallis (1793)|Thomas Munro (1820)|
|Implemented In|North & Central India|Bengal, Bihar, Odisha|South & West India|
|Revenue Settlement|With **village (Mahal)**|With **Zamindars**|With **individual peasants (Ryots)**|
|Revenue Demand|**Flexible, revised periodically**|**Fixed permanently**|**Flexible, revised periodically**|
|Responsibility|**Village headmen (Lambardar)**|**Zamindars (Landlords)**|**Individual farmers (Ryots)**|

## **Conclusion**

The Mahalwari System was introduced to improve revenue collection but ended up being **oppressive for peasants** due to **high taxes and corruption**. It was **one of the three major land revenue systems** of British India, but like the others, it **favored the British and landlords** over farmers.
