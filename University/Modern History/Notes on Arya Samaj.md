#### 1. **Introduction to Arya Samaj**
   - **Founder**: Swami Dayanand Saraswati (1824–1883).
   - **Year of Establishment**: 1875 in Bombay (now Mumbai).
   - **Motto**: "Krinvanto Vishwam Aryam" (Make the World Noble).
   - **Core Objective**: To reform Hinduism and promote Vedic principles.
---

   ### ***Founder: Swami Dayanand Saraswati**

- Born as **Mool Shankar** in **1824** in Gujarat.
- Left home at an early age in search of truth.
- Deeply influenced by the **Vedas** and sought to revive **Vedic principles**.
- Opposed **idol worship, caste discrimination, untouchability, and rituals**.
- Wrote **Satyarth Prakash** (The Light of Truth), explaining his thoughts.
- Died in **1883** due to poisoning.

#### 2. **Key Principles of Arya Samaj**
   - **Ten Principles (Dasa Niyams)**:
     1. God is the primary source of all true knowledge.
     2. God is formless, omnipotent, and eternal.
     3. The Vedas are the true scriptures of knowledge.
     4. Truth should be accepted and falsehood rejected.
     5. All actions should be performed in accordance with Dharma (righteousness).
     6. The primary aim of Arya Samaj is to do good for all humanity.
     7. Everyone should be treated with love, fairness, and justice.
     8. Ignorance should be eradicated, and knowledge should be promoted.
     9. One should not be content with one’s progress alone but should work for the upliftment of others.
     10. Social well-being should be prioritized over individual interests.

#### 3. **Beliefs and Practices**
   - **Vedic Authority**: Arya Samaj considers the Vedas as the ultimate authority and rejects later Hindu texts like Puranas and Tantras.
   - **Monotheism**: Belief in one formless God.
   - **Rejection of Idol Worship**: Arya Samaj opposes idol worship and rituals.
   - **Emphasis on Education**: Promotes Vedic education and modern scientific knowledge.
   - **Social Reforms**: Advocates for the abolition of caste discrimination, child marriage, and untouchability.
   - **Women’s Rights**: Supports women’s education and equal rights.
   - **Yajnas (Fire Rituals)**: Performs Vedic yajnas for spiritual and social purposes.

#### 4. **Social Reforms Initiated by Arya Samaj**
   - **Abolition of Caste System**: Opposed caste-based discrimination and promoted social equality.
   - **Women Empowerment**: Advocated for women’s education and widow remarriage.
   - **Opposition to Superstitions**: Rejected blind faith, rituals, and superstitions.
   - **Promotion of Hindi**: Encouraged the use of Hindi as a unifying language.
   - **Shuddhi Movement**: Reconversion of individuals who had converted to other religions back to Hinduism.

#### 5. **Educational Contributions**
   - **Establishment of Schools and Colleges**: Founded numerous educational institutions, including Dayanand Anglo-Vedic (DAV) schools and colleges.
   - **Gurukul System**: Revived the ancient Gurukul system of education, emphasizing Vedic teachings alongside modern subjects.
   - **Literacy Campaigns**: Promoted literacy and education among all sections of society.

#### 6. **Religious and Philosophical Contributions**
   - **Satyarth Prakash**: Swami Dayanand’s seminal work, which critiques other religions and presents the philosophy of Arya Samaj.
   - **Vedic Interpretation**: Emphasized the rational and scientific interpretation of the Vedas.
   - **Critique of Other Religions**: Criticized practices in Islam, Christianity, and other religions while advocating for a return to Vedic principles.

#### 7. **Organizational Structure**
   - **Local Branches (Sabhas)**: Arya Samaj has numerous local branches across India and abroad.
   - **Leadership**: Managed by elected representatives and scholars.
   - **Activities**: Regular gatherings for Vedic teachings, yajnas, and social service.

#### 8. **Impact of Arya Samaj**
   - **Revival of Hinduism**: Played a significant role in reviving Hinduism during the colonial era.
   - **Social Awakening**: Contributed to the Indian freedom struggle by fostering national pride and unity.
   - **Global Presence**: Spread to countries with Indian diaspora, including the USA, UK, Canada, and South Africa.

#### 9. **Criticism and Controversies**
   - **Critique of Other Religions**: - Faced resistance from **Muslims and Christians** due to the **Shuddhi Movement**.
   - **Orthodox Opposition**: Faced opposition from orthodox Hindus for rejecting idol worship and caste system.
   - British authorities disliked Arya Samaj’s **nationalistic approach**
   - **Internal Divisions**: Differences in interpretation of Vedic texts have led to internal divisions.

#### 10. **Legacy of Arya Samaj**
   - **Influence on Indian Society**: Played a key role in shaping modern Indian society by promoting education, social equality, and rational thinking.
   - **Inspiration for Leaders**: Influenced leaders like **Lala Lajpat Rai, Swami Shraddhanand, and Madan Mohan Malaviya.**
   - Promoted **Swadeshi (self-reliance) and self-governance**.
   - **Continued Relevance**: Arya Samaj remains active today, promoting Vedic values and social reforms.

#### 11. **Key Figures Associated with Arya Samaj**
   - **Swami Dayanand Saraswati**: Founder and spiritual leader.
   - **Lala Lajpat Rai**: Freedom fighter and prominent Arya Samaji.
   - **Swami Shraddhanand**: Leader of the Shuddhi Movement.
   - **Mahatma Hansraj**: Key figure in establishing DAV institutions.

#### 12. **Arya Samaj in Modern Times**
   - **Global Outreach**: Continues to spread Vedic teachings globally.
   - **Social Service**: Engages in humanitarian activities like disaster relief and education.
   - **Adaptation to Modernity**: Balances traditional Vedic values with contemporary issues.

#### 13. **Conclusion**
   - Arya Samaj is a reformist movement that seeks to restore the purity of Vedic teachings while addressing social issues.
   - It has made significant contributions to education, social equality, and the revival of Hinduism.
   - Despite controversies, it remains a vital force in promoting rational thinking and spiritual growth.
